requirement · opt-in curator badge

Earned by people, not points

The badge unlocks only when multiple distinct friends mark your sends “so me.” You can’t farm it by blasting videos, because credit comes only from a real person’s genuine reaction.

  • Comparison-free: it marks quality of taste, never a rank against others
  • Opt-in display, off until you flip the switch
  • Framed as recognition, so the moment feels like a gift, not a scoreboard
positive-only · private by default
requirement · curator identity on profile

An identity that isn’t follower count

For the roughly half of users who never post, the profile has always been empty. Now taste is the thing on display: how often your picks land, and who you’re the go-to sender for.

  • Curator badge sits next to the name once opted in
  • “Your taste landed 47 times this month,” a warm private stat
  • “Most-vibed sender for” names real friendships, not a leaderboard

requirement · group “meme-god”

A crown you can lose, so it stays fun

Inside a group chat, a little bragging-rights competition is in-joke culture. The trick that keeps it healthy: it’s celebratory and rotating, a monthly nod you can be dethroned from, not a permanent hierarchy.

  • Group-scoped and opt-in, the safest home for the competitive energy
  • Playful descriptors over cold ranks (“niche cat content dealer”)
  • Counts are warm hearts, never a public global score
celebration, not competition
requirement · per-chat shared archive

Every video you two ever traded

Open a friend’s chat and there’s a running history of your shared taste, the inside jokes and finds that define the friendship. Retrieval that TikTok is famously bad at, made effortless.

  • A “Shared” tab beside the conversation, per friend
  • The ones you marked “so me” keep their heart
  • Becomes a memory lane, not a dead scroll of gray bubbles
